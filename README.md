# Psychohistory in the Age of AI: The Algorithm for Calculating Social Disruptions and Bending the Future

**Author:** Emin   

---

Asimov’s "Psychohistory" from the *Foundation* series —the science of predicting the behavioral trends of billions of people using mathematical formulas— has long been viewed as a sci-fi fantasy. Traditional sociology and econometrics have consistently failed to predict non-linear social phase transitions (revolutions, collapses, sudden ideological shifts) because they could not quantify the asymmetric and dynamic nature of the human spirit, beliefs, and collective emotions.

Yet today, we stand right in the middle of local large language models (LLMs), multi-agent architectures, and real-time big data analysis. As a systems engineer, I assert: **Psychohistory is no longer fiction; it is a calculable, improvable, and deterministic science.** Furthermore, in an era of mass unemployment, stagflation, and the resulting anti-AI radical populism triggered by the uncontrolled growth of artificial intelligence, it is the only steering wheel we have to protect the digital world and social peace.

Mass unemployment driven by AI automation will not just clog economic systems; the marginalized and enraged masses will propel radical populist politicians to power. Even more dangerously, unemployed computer geniuses could leverage autonomous AI-powered cyber viruses on local machines to dismantle the entire digital landscape and financial infrastructure. Before entering this "Digital Disruption" phase, we must anticipate systemic balances and redirect social energy into constructive channels.

How do we build a World Psychohistory Model using AI? What should be the developmental steps and the resulting systemic formula? Let's construct this framework using empirical lessons gathered from the field.
