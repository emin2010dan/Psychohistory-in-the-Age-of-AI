## Introduction: Why Now?

Isaac Asimov, in his *Foundation* series written in 1951, envisioned a fictional science called "psychohistory." This science would predict and even guide the future of societies through mathematical modeling. Asimov's dream seemed impossible at the time — because the necessary computational power, data, and theoretical framework simply did not exist.

Half a century later, three things have changed:

1. **Computational Power**: Artificial intelligence can process trillions of data points in seconds.

2. **Data Access**: The digital age produces more data about human behavior than ever before in history.

3. **Neural Networks**: Pattern recognition can capture cycles and relationships in historical data at a depth that humans could never achieve.

In this article, we will discuss how the science of psychohistory can be developed with the help of artificial intelligence, which parameters should be included in the formula, and why this science is not just a "future prediction" tool, but also **the most powerful tool for shaping the future.**

## Chapter 1: Pattern Mining — Why Does History Repeat?

### 1.1 Fundamental Observation

History is not a random sequence of events. Under certain conditions, societies follow certain patterns. Peter Turchin's "cliodynamics" model, Ibn Khaldun's "asabiyyah" theory, Glubb's "empire life cycles," and Kondratiev waves — all point to the same truth: **Societies pass through recognizable stages.**

### 1.2 Historical Patterns

There are three main pattern families:

**A. Empire Collapse Cycle (7 Stages)**

| Stage | Feature | Indicator |
|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Military overextension | Military spending/GDP ratio > 5% |
| 2 | Currency devaluation | Acceleration of money supply expansion |
| 3 | Debt spiral | Interest payments > Military spending |
| 4 | Loss of productive capacity | Decline in import/export ratio |
| 5 | Social decay | Trust in institutions < 30% |
| 6 | Loss of reserve currency status | Increase in use of alternative currencies |
| 7 | Sudden collapse | Systemic crisis (within 900 days) |

**B. Revolution Cycle (6 Stages)**

| Stage | Feature | Historical Example |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | Extreme wealth inequality | France 1789: Top 1% = 40% of wealth |
| 2 | Government incompetence | Trust in elections < 20% |
| 3 | Middle class radicalization | "System cannot be fixed" perception |
| 4 | Distrust of institutions | Courts = "political" perception |
| 5 | Revolutionary movement goes mass | Alternative promise = strengthening |
| 6 | Chaos | 10+ years of civil war, dictatorship |

**C. Hegemony Loss Cycle**

Portugal (1500) → Netherlands (1650) → England (1815) → USA (1944) → ?

Every hegemony passes through the same stages: **Military dominance → Trade surplus → Formalization of status → Deficit spending → Loss of reserve → New hegemon.**

### 1.3 Failed Patterns = Control Group

What makes psychohistory powerful is not just the analysis of "successful" patterns, but the analysis of **failed patterns**:

| Country | Breakpoint Intervention | Result |
|---------|------------------------|--------|
| England | Industrial loss → Financial center | Slow decline, middle power |
| Germany (1923) | Reliable reference (rent index) | Hyperinflation broken |
| Japan | Zero interest + housewife carry trade | "Lost decade" but survival |

**Conclusion**: Passing through the same pattern ≠ Inevitable collapse. **Correct intervention = Breakpoint.**
