## Introduction: Why Now?

Artificial intelligence today can hold conversations with individuals, produce art, and write code. But the world it inhabits is a society. A system that understands individual psychology cannot see where society is heading. This is like trying to understand a fish while ignoring the ocean.

The concept of **psychohistory** that Isaac Asimov created in his *Foundation* series says, at its core: individuals are unpredictable, but sufficiently large masses are statistically foreseeable. You cannot know where a single molecule of gas will go, but you can calculate the gas's pressure, temperature, and volume.

The days when artificial intelligences will become part of human society are no longer a fantasy. For this integration to happen in a healthy way, artificial intelligences must understand society. And to understand society, a science like psychohistory is necessary.

In this article, we discuss how the science of psychohistory can be developed with the help of artificial intelligence, what parameters it might consist of, and why this work is an urgent necessity.

## 1. Known Patterns: Starting Points

Psychohistory does not start from a vacuum. Throughout history, various scholars have independently discovered societal patterns:

- **Ibn Khaldun's Asabiyyah Theory:** Waves of social solidarity and the roughly 3–4 generational rise-and-fall cycle of empires
- **Kondratiev Waves:** 40–60 year economic growth-contraction cycles
- **Peter Turchin's Cliodynamics:** A mathematical model of political instability
- **Strauss-Howe Generational Theory:** Approximately 80-year cycles of four generations
- **Hegemonic Cycles:** 100–150 year cycles of leading power rise and fall (Portugal → Netherlands → England → USA → ?)
- **Schumpeter's Creative Destruction:** The absorption-saturation-crisis phases of technological paradigms

However, these models share a common problem: **they are seen looking backward.** The true purpose of psychohistory is real-time phase detection and forward-looking projection.

**The primary goal: How can we detect new patterns?**

## 2. Methodological Foundation: Wave Upon Wave

### 2.1 Life Does Not Flow Linearly

Societal data is not linear — it is wave-like. But the wavelength of each subject is different: population growth, technology adoption, economic cycles, and cultural transformations all move at different frequencies. The combination of these waves constitutes the chaos data of life.

The path to seeing the future:
1. Determine what the waves are and calculate their frequency and amplitude
2. Calculate by projection what the values of these waves will be in the future
3. Recombine the calculated wave values

> **Critical methodological note:** When analyzing wave-shaped data, do not use rectified activation functions such as ReLU. Instead, use oscillation formulas:

```
f(t) = A · sin(2π/T · t + φ) · e^(αt)
```

Here `α > 0` represents the growth phase and `α < 0` represents the decline phase. This formula carries both the cycle and the long-term trend together.

### 2.2 Detecting New Patterns: The Residual Signal Method

Take a complex historical time series. After subtracting the known waves (Kondratiev, Strauss-Howe, hegemonic cycles), the **residual** that remains is the area where new patterns are hidden:

```
New_Pattern ≈ Actual_Data - Σ(Known_Wave_Models)
```

### 2.3 Leading Indicator Detection: Cross-Correlation

Which signals were consistently rising 20–30 years before each known phase (for example, "military overextension")? By calculating cross-correlations across all historical examples, a **set of leading indicators** for each phase can be extracted.

### 2.4 Phase Detection: The Most Critical Question

The true value of psychohistory lies not in naming a pattern, but in knowing **which phase of that pattern we are currently in**. The right question is not "Is this a collapse?" but "What phase are we in right now?"

## 3. The Medium Variable: Context, Not Time

### 3.1 Waves Behave According to Their Medium

An ocean wave behaves differently in deep water than in shallow water. The same energy produces different results in different mediums. For historical cycles, the "medium" is equally determinative:

```
Medium (m) = Institutional Flexibility × Speed of Information Propagation × Economic Diversity
```

When we use medium-based measurement instead of time-based measurement, the formula becomes:

```
f(m) = A · sin(2π/λ · m + φ) · e^(αm)
```

### 3.2 Historical Examples

- **England:** When the industrial wave broke, it changed its medium. By making London the center of global finance, it channeled its energy into a different canal. It did not collapse — it transformed.
- **Germany (1923):** Against the hyperinflation cycle, it printed the Rentenmark backed by a fixed value such as land. By producing a counter-frequency signal, it achieved destructive interference.
- **Japan:** It lowered interest rates to zero, but the demographic wave (aging population) was operating simultaneously. Since the two waves ran at different frequencies, the system experienced neither full collapse nor full recovery — it fell into a resonance trap.

> **Psychohistory principle:** Measure not when a pattern will occur, but under what medium conditions it will occur.

## 4. The Emotional Mode Parameter

### 4.1 People Make Emotional Decisions, Not Rational Ones

Game theory and rational actor models are valid for only a small minority. Human communities make largely emotional decisions and use reason to find evidence supporting those decisions. Every psychohistory model that does not account for this will systematically produce incorrect results.

### 4.2 Three Societal Emotional Modes

Societies exist in three fundamental emotional modes depending on environmental conditions:

| Mode | Trigger | Dominant Figure | Decision Engine | Sacrifice |
|------|---------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Mode 1: Crisis** | Economic collapse, war | Hero / Dictator | Collective survival | Very easy |
| **Mode 2: Order** | Post-crisis stability | Lawmaker / Clergy | Rules and predictability | Harder |
| **Mode 3: Prosperity** | Prolonged peace | Entrepreneur / Profiteer | Individual gain | Very difficult |

This cycle operates as nested large waves (30-year) and small waves (3-year). A society in Mode 2 can exhibit temporary Mode 1 behavior during a brief economic shock, then return to its primary mode.

### 4.3 How Do We Measure the Mode?

**Mode 1 signals:** Political polarization index, collapse of institutional trust, war/sacrifice language in media, surveys on demand for strong leadership

**Mode 2 signals:** Surge in new laws and regulations, rise in savings rate, recovery of institutional trust

**Mode 3 signals:** Rising Gini coefficient, growing consumer debt, deteriorating corruption index, density of wealth/celebrity content in media

## 5. Cultural Code: The Viscosity of the Medium

### 5.1 Why Does the Same Wave Produce Different Results?

If a formula works in one country but collapses in another, the missing variable is the cultural code. Cultural code is the society's **viscosity** in the face of a wave — thick molasses and helium gas are both theoretically fluid, but their diffusion coefficients are entirely different.

### 5.2 Historical Evidence

**China and the Open Ocean:** It had the technology, the philosophers, the enormous fleet. A single imperial edict ended everything. Why? Because in China's cultural code, obedience to central authority comes before individual or collective desire for exploration.

**The Mongols:** The most powerful war machine, but could not build a lasting administrative structure. Cultural code: plunder and movement, not settlement and governance.

**The Ottomans and the Atlantic:** They held dominion over the Mediterranean but did not open up to the Atlantic. Their legitimacy was tied to the Byzantine heritage and the Silk Road; the Atlantic was outside this narrative.

**The Turks:** Not the most intelligent, not the most powerful — but the most adaptive. The pattern of migrating from lands at -72 degrees to everywhere, starting as mercenaries and rising to governance, puts cultural viscosity into numbers. The fact that Turkey's private gold reserves exceed 5,000 tons (World Gold Council) is the cumulative historical score of distrust toward central government — cultural DNA read from a single data point.

### 5.3 Measuring Cultural Code

| Component | Measurement Data |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Migration coefficient | What percentage of the population migrates under pressure? |
| Radius of trust | To whom do people lend money or form partnerships? |
| Institutional vs. relational loyalty | Size of the informal economy, rate of nepotism in employment |
| Risk tolerance | Rate of entrepreneurship, speed of technology adoption |
| Time horizon | Savings rate, investment in children's education |
| Ideological flexibility | Rate of change in religious practices over time |

### 5.4 Cultural Code Change: The Bell Curve Model

If we model society as a single homogeneous block, simulations will turn out to be incorrect. Cultural change operates as a distribution:

```
Cultural_Distribution = N(μ, σ²)
μ = the code of the society's central mass (changes very slowly)
σ = the society's internal diversity
```

The energetic and convinced fringes (the 10–15% in the tails) initiate change. The majority shifts only after these pioneers reach critical mass and the cost of the old code exceeds the cost of the new one.

**Forcibly applied change** creates tension between the legal code and the behavioral code:

```
Cultural_Tension(t) = |Legal_Code(t) - Behavioral_Code(t)|
```

This tension pushes the system toward either genuine transformation or a restoration wave — Turkey oscillates between these two outcomes.

## 6. The Cohort Model: Same People, Different Behaviors

The reason people react differently to the same event 30 years apart is the decay of generational memory:

- **Lived memory (0–30 years):** Bodily memory — reflex-based behavior
- **Narrative memory (30–80 years):** Story — the lesson exists, but the emotion has been lost
- **Historical memory (80+ years):** Text only — behavioral coding has been reset; the cycle can begin again

```
Behavioral_Effect(t) = E₀ · e^(-λt)
```

`λ` = the decay coefficient of memory. Major traumas decay slowly; the cultural transmission mechanism determines the speed of decay.

**Cohort modeling:** Divide the population into age cohorts and label each cohort with the ideological climate of the period that shaped it.

| Duration (educational effect) | Affected Group |
|-------------------------------|---------------|
| 11 years (ages 7–17) | Youth |
| 22 years (ages 7–28) | Productive young adults |
| 33 years (ages 7–39) | Active producers |
| Age 40+ | Resistance to change begins |

**Ideological contamination:** An actor who tries to spread an idea by force erodes that idea's credibility with every negative action:

```
Ideology_Credibility(t) = G₀ · e^(-λ · Negative_Actions(t))
```

**Variance compression:** Removing creative and nonconformist individuals from the system reduces the society's standard deviation:

```
σ_after = σ_before × (1 - Brain_Drain_Rate)ⁿ
```

A smaller σ means a more predictable but more fragile society. In historical breaking points, it is usually that outlying 5% that saves the system.

## 7. The Motivation Parameter: Potential Energy

### 7.1 It Is Motivation, Not Technology, That Determines

China had built great ocean-going ships and had sailed as far as Africa — but did not follow through. Spain sailed much farther into unknown waters with far smaller ships. The problem was not in the technology, but in the magnitude of the society's motivation.

Motivation behaves like **potential energy** in physics:

```
Motivation(society, domain) = (Desired_State - Current_State) / Current_Resistance
```

### 7.2 The Pressure Cooker Dynamic

Motivation accumulates and breaks through at the point of least resistance — not necessarily in the expected direction. Viking motivation was real but scattered and institutionally unsupported. Puritan motivation was real and also channeled by the community institution:

```
Lasting_Impact = Motivation × Institutional_Channeling
```

### 7.3 Projection to the Present Day

**China + Nuclear:** Hormuz restrictions → energy supply deficit → high motivation + existing nuclear capacity + strong state channeling → high probability of advancement in fission/fusion within the next 10 years.

**USA + Robotics:** Tariffs + expensive labor + immigration reduction policy → deliberate pressure increase → acceleration of industrial robotics and automation.

### 7.4 Measuring Motivation

| Indicator | What It Measures |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Sudden surge in sectoral patent applications | Direction of motivation |
| Migration patterns of researchers in that domain | Lack of opportunity |
| Startup formation sectors | The gap the market senses |
| Energy consumption (by R&D facility) | Actual intensity of work |

## 8. The Hope Parameter: The Direction of Motivation

### 8.1 Motivation + Hope = Constructive Force

Motivation generates pressure like a pressure cooker, but **without hope, this force becomes destructive rather than developmental for society.** This can be clearly observed in Turkey in the flight of valuable minds, capital, and factories abroad.

Hope is different from optimism:

> **Hope = "My belief that the effort I invest today will produce a meaningful result within my own lifetime"**

```
Hope(society, t) = P(future_is_better) × Institutional_Credibility × Effort_Reward_Link
```

### 8.2 The Aging Society Trap

```
Hope_Suppression(t) = Median_Age(t) × Elderly_Voting_Weight(t)
```

As the median age rises, the electorate's interests shift from the future to the present. Investment in the labor power of the young falls to the background; protection of existing wealth comes to the fore.

### 8.3 Hope Indicators (Non-Manipulable)

| Indicator | Direction |
|-----------|-----------|
| Rate of skilled emigration | Negative |
| Capital flight | Negative |
| Proportion of young people among patent applicants | Positive |
| Average age of startup founders | Positive if low |
| Popular fiction genres (dystopia dominance) | Negative |
| Age of marriage (becoming younger) | Positive |
| Rate of dark humor on social media | Negative |

## 9. Data Methodology: Non-Manipulable Data

The most critical principle in selecting psychohistory data is: **Measure not what people say, but what they do.**

There can be enormous gaps between a country's official statistics and reality. Smart economists look not at government data but at market data. According to an anecdote, *a retired economist would take the sales figures of a luxury pickle producer and base his economic predictions on it* — because this data could not be corrupted by those in power.

**Examples of behavioral fingerprints:**

| Signal | What It Reveals |
|--------|----------------|
| Declining birth rate | Insecurity about the future |
| Precious metal purchases | Distrust in the currency |
| Nighttime satellite photographs (light intensity) | Real economic activity |
| VPN download numbers | Political pressure |
| Brain drain | Distrust in the system |
| Second-hand market activity | Real purchasing power |

**Accuracy coefficient for historical data:** History is a narrative written by the victors. Assign each source a confidence coefficient based on the following factors:

```
Confidence(source) = f(number_of_independent_sources, author_position, overlap_with_material_evidence)
```

## 10. Black Swans and Manufactured Triggers

Nassim Taleb says black swans are by definition unforeseeable. However, viewed from the psychohistory perspective, the picture is different: **As the system approaches the threshold of rupture, powerful actors who notice this manufacture their own triggers.**

Historical examples: The US oil embargo on Japan in the lead-up to Pearl Harbor, the SS dressing in Polish uniforms at the Gleiwitz incident, the Gulf of Tonkin affair with the listening vessel...

Within this framework, the right question is:

> Not "When will the black swan arrive?" but **"When will conditions ripen sufficiently that an actor finds it profitable to paint a white swan black?"**

```
Manufactured_Trigger_Probability(t) = f(System_Stress(t), Actor_Gain, Cultural_Acceptance_Threshold)
```

## 11. The Master Formula: The Complete Psychohistory Equation

When we bring all the components together:

```
Societal_Output(t, K) = 
  Primary_Wave(m) × Cultural_Viscosity(K)
  + Σ Secondary_Waves_i × Emotional_Mode(t)
  + [Motivation(domain) × Institutional_Channeling] / Resistance(t)
  + Hope(t) × Effort_Reward_Link
  - Cultural_Tension(t) × Restoration_Coefficient
```

**The role of each term:**

- `Primary_Wave(m)` → Long-term historical cycle (expressed in medium units)
- `Cultural_Viscosity(K)` → The society's resistance to change and movement
- `Secondary_Waves` → Short-term sub-cycles (economic, political shocks)
- `Emotional_Mode(t)` → Whether the society is in Mode 1, 2, or 3
- `Motivation × Institutional_Channeling` → Directed energy of change
- `Hope × Effort_Reward_Link` → Whether the outcome will be constructive or destructive
- `Cultural_Tension × Restoration_Coefficient` → The rebound force of forcibly imposed change

## 12. Validation Methodology

Psychohistory is a new science; the correctness of a formula cannot be determined simply by looking at it. Validation is carried out through numerical analysis methods:

1. Collect historical data with accuracy coefficients
2. Apply a specific cross-section of this data to the formula
3. Compare the output with what actually happened
4. If there is a large discrepancy, identify the missing variable and update the formula
5. Repeat the cycle

Do not expect a perfect formula — historical data is corrupted. Recent history offers cleaner data but contains incomplete patterns. Use it only for testing the phases of completed cycles.

1. **Projection:** Calculating the probable future with current parameters
2. **Intervention design:** Showing at what point and through what kind of intervention the wavelength can be shortened

This is precisely what Asimov's Hari Seldon was doing: not stopping the destruction, but **shortening the waiting period** through the right intervention at the right point.
